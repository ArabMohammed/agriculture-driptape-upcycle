The problem

A durable polymer, thrown away after one season

Modern agriculture relies heavily on drip irrigation. After their short operational life, thin drip tapes become agricultural waste despite being made from durable polymers designed for the outdoors.

Their short life is not the plastic wearing out, but the tiny emitter openings clogging up. Each tape releases water through microscopic holes and narrow labyrinth channels, easily blocked by fine soil particles and mineral scale, mainly limestone (calcium carbonate) that precipitates from the irrigation water. Once clogged, the tape is pulled out and discarded rather than cleaned, even though the polymer is still strong.

On emitter clogging, see Irritec and CNPandora.

Our solution

Upcycling, not recycling

Conventional recycling shreds end-of-life tapes and melts them into pellets, which is energy-intensive and destroys the durable structure the material already has. ReWeave keeps that structure intact.

How it works

From waste to a working product

Gather end-of-life tapes that would otherwise be discarded or burned.

2

Clean and cut the tapes into uniform strips, ready to work by hand.

3

Weave

Interlace the strips into a dense, durable mat with a plain one-over-one weave.

4

Deploy

Lay the mats to shade soil, cut evaporation, suppress weeds, and resist erosion.

Inside the weave

The mat uses a plain one-over-one (1/1) weave, where each warp crosses a single weft at a time. This is chosen deliberately: it features the maximum number of interlacing crossover points, making the mat very stable, ravel-resistant, and less prone to snagging.

Drip tape is flat and flexible rather than stiff like wire, so the weft strips tend to slip out of position. To lock them in, we punch holes through the warp strips along each edge and thread the weft through them, anchoring every row while keeping the polymer fully intact.

Impact

Every square metre counts

2.0 kg

CO₂ emissions avoided per square metre of mat

640 g

Agricultural plastic diverted from disposal per square metre

3+ yrs

Proven outdoor durability in prototype validation

A single square metre of ReWeave mat is woven from over 80 metres of reclaimed drip tape. At roughly 8 grams per metre, that adds up to about 640 grams of agricultural plastic rescued from disposal in every mat.

Drip tape is made from low-density polyethylene (LDPE), whose repeat unit is about 86% carbon. When burned, that carbon oxidises into CO₂, releasing about 3.1 kg per kilogram. By upcycling the tape instead, each square metre prevents roughly 2.0 kg of CO₂ emissions.

At scale: every 1,000 m² of deployed mats keeps around 640 kg of plastic in use and prevents roughly 2.0 tonnes of CO₂ from entering the atmosphere.

The tape weighs about 8 g/m and we weave over 80 m into each square metre, both measured from our own prototypes (outdoors since 2023). The CO₂ figure is specific to polyethylene (~86% carbon, ~3.1 kg CO₂/kg on complete combustion; see WWF Australia and Bioprocess Tools). Open burning is less complete, so we treat this as an upper estimate.

Perspectives

What we are working toward

ReWeave is still early, and our current prototypes are hand-woven. These directions turn the process into an affordable, scalable product.

Automated weaving

Machinery to automate the weave, cutting production time and cost so mats are affordable at field scale.

Field collection machines

Machines that gather used tape and wind it into rolls, so collection pays for itself.

Wire-stitched edges

Replace punched-hole edge-locking with wire-stitched edges, better suited to automation.

Less material per mat

A more material-efficient weave that slices the weft lengthwise, cutting tape used by about 25%.

Farmer pilots

Field pilots to quantify water savings, weed suppression, and yield effects.

Erosion-control market

A durable, locally made alternative to imported mats for slopes, embankments, and channels.
